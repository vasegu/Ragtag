# Available resources

Version 0.1.0 · Research date: **1 November 2026**.

This document contains the findings, all 37 resource records, their 21 sources,
limitations and the operational data request. No companion files are required.

Public information establishes funded services, staffing establishments, planned
funding and purchasing routes. It does **not** establish today's spare crews,
spendable balance, stock or contractor slots. The register deliberately leaves
`live_available_quantity` null throughout. Unknown is not zero or unlimited.

describes **15 additional sweepers**, **four additional fly-tipping removal crews
on top of 16**, and enforcement expansion **from two to eight officers**. Thus
20 removal crews is an announced post-expansion total, not verified November
availability. Do not infer an exact sweeper baseline from “almost 25%”, assume
the additional recruitment happened, or count enforcement officers as removal crews.

The [2026/27 budget report](https://hackney.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s96964/2026_27%20Budget%20Report.pdf)
describes £13.6m for highways-related services, £23.5m for cleansing/waste
collection and £7.6m for parks/green spaces/leisure. These are broad annual
service spending descriptions. They are not distinct pots of uncommitted cash.

The [highways transparency report](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1koXHulZSzkO6hLl8Z0SEnbs0ElL5dnEElSYwIeRZ_fc/edit)
projects **£8,169,252** maintenance spending for 2026/27. Its carriageway, footway,
structures and drainage components sum to **£8,220,452**, a **£51,200 difference**.
Do not silently reconcile it or combine it with the broader £13.6m figure. The
report describes an annual gully programme and asset/work records in Alloy,
Earthlight and Mayrise, useful routes to operational data rather than extra crews.

[DfT allocations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/highways-maintenance-funding-allocations/highways-maintenance-block-formula-allocations-2026-to-2030)

This is a funding source already reflected in the highways report, not another
£1.089m to add to that report's total. Check receipt, conditions and commitments.

## What the allocator can use now

Use the public register to define **resource types and capability constraints**:
highway inspection, civil repair, surfacing, electrical lighting, gully cleansing,
arboriculture, cleansing/removal, enforcement, structural inspection and housing
trades. Keep action tasks such as inspect, make safe and permanently repair
separate, as in the severity model.

Use published annual figures for context and scenario checks. A real schedule
needs verified capacity over its actual planning window. Do not turn FTE into
available hours by multiplying by an assumed working week. If a resource is
unknown, flag the affected task as capacity-unconfirmed and retain it in the
backlog; do not treat it as safely schedulable or impossible forever.

Council resources, contracted capacity and external handoffs are different:
TfL, Thames Water and other asset owners cannot be scheduled as council crews.
Record the handoff/acceptance and its separate response commitment instead.

## Minimum operational data to request

| Responsible service | Needed data for the planning window |
|---|---|
| Streetscene / highway contractor manager | Inspector and repair crew IDs, skills/certification, crew composition, shift start/end, depot, booked work, on-call reserve, travel/setup time, accepted call-offs and rates |
| Environmental Operations | Sweeper/removal/gully team rotas, ward commitments, residual shift capacity, waste handling restrictions, tip locations/opening hours and disposal charges |
| Finance / service budget owners | Cost-centre/funding IDs, eligible work, current authorised budget, actual spend, commitments, separately held reserves, approved transfers, recoverable VAT treatment and remaining authority to spend |
| Fleet / depots | Vehicle/plant type, attachments, operational status, driver qualification, service appointments, duty allocation, fuel/charge, load capacity and depot access |
| Stores / suppliers | Usable material quantities, unit, location, existing reservations, replenishment lead times, shelf life and compatible asset/material specifications |
| Arboriculture / parks / grounds | In-house and supplier availability, competent climbers/operators, required plant, tree permissions, safe weather limits and seasonal work commitments |
| Building Control / Housing Repairs | Duty surveyor/trade capacity, access constraints, scheduled jobs, specialist contractors and eligible budget ownership |
| Network management | Confirmed permit/closure windows, traffic-management crews, streetworks restrictions, joint-working opportunities and public-access requirements |

These are a proposed data request, not a message sent to the council. Obtain
team-level data first; personal employee details are unnecessary for the demo.

Recommended operational record fields:

```text
resource_id, resource_type, service_owner, provider, capabilities,
quantity, unit, available_from, available_until, already_committed,
emergency_reserve, location_or_depot, eligible_work, funding_pool_id,
cost_basis, dependencies, evidence_source, verified_at, valid_until
```

For time-based resources, compute usable capacity from verified shift time less
bookings, breaks, travel/setup and reserves **in the same units**, without counting
one crew's component staff as additional independent capacity. For budgets,
reconcile actuals and commitments before computing a remaining balance. An
unverified overlapping annual budget is not a substitute for that calculation.

## Research limits

Public evidence has not established current highway crew counts, daily productive
hours, stores inventories, individual vehicle availability, contractor spare slots,
or uncommitted November budget. Staffing is budgeted establishment; announcements
are plans until delivery is verified. No fictional operational quantities have
been filled into this register, and no allocator or external integrations were built.
