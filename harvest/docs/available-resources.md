# Available resources

Version 0.2.0 · Research and assumption review: **1 November 2026**.

This document contains 37 public resource records, 20 delivery-capability profiles,
24 sources, explicit planning assumptions and the cost inputs for deciding how
to deliver a repair. No companion files are required.

Public information establishes funded services, staffing establishments, planned
funding and purchasing routes. It does **not** establish today's spare crews,
spendable balance, stock or contractor slots. The register deliberately leaves
`live_available_quantity` null throughout. Unknown is not zero or unlimited.

The capability catalogue below adds resources Hackney can plausibly draw on
through direct delivery, existing contracts, hire or further procurement.
**Documented access, assumed access and confirmed bookable capacity are separate
states.** Assumed resources are available for scenario analysis only until verified.

## In-house, contracted and additional delivery options

Compare the delivery of a **specified action**—inspection, making safe or permanent
repair—not an entire report without a scope. A contractor doing council-owned
work does not change the council's service responsibility. A referral to Thames
Water or TfL for its own asset is not council outsourcing and must not be costed
as buying a council crew.

| Mode | What it means | What must be confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| I: existing in-house | Qualified council staff using available council equipment | Skills, safe team composition, free shift, plant, materials and eligible funding |
| T: existing contract | Call-off/work order to the current term, support or framework provider | Current contract/extension, scope, order authority, rates, accepted slot and any minimum charge |
| H: supplemented in-house | Council delivery with authorised overtime, agency labour, hired plant or specialist subcontract support | Recruitment/hire route, supervision, competence, lead time and additional cost |
| X: additional outsourcing | A new supplier or additional package not already secured by T | Compliant purchasing route, competition/approval as applicable, specification, mobilisation and accepted quote |
| R: external-owner referral | Responsible utility, TfL, landlord or other owner arranges its own work | Ownership, accepted handoff and separate council safety/enforcement tasks; excluded from I/T/H/X cost comparison |

Purchasing routes are not interchangeable. A framework place is not a booked
crew, and an expired or out-of-scope contract cannot be treated as a valid T option.
Client-side inspection, authorisation, legal decisions and acceptance remain
council responsibilities unless a specific lawful delegation/contract says otherwise.

## Assumptions and costs for an in-house-versus-outsourced analysis

### Evidence and assumption states

For every task/mode input use `observed`, `approved_estimate`,
`planning_assumption` or `unknown`, with source/owner, date, units and validity.
Document why an assumption is plausible and what confirms it. A low/base/high
range can be used for a demo; it must never be relabelled an accepted quote or
bookable slot. Capacity remains null unless a separate **synthetic scenario**
provides explicit fictional values. This document adds no assumed live crew totals.

### Inputs required for each feasible delivery mode

| Input | In-house I / supplemented H | Existing contract T / additional outsourcing X |
|---|---|---|
| Specification and outcome | Exact remedial scope, safety controls, quality standard and acceptance | Same scope/outcome; include exclusions and who carries residual work |
| Timing | Earliest safe start, elapsed completion and productive staff hours | Accepted mobilisation/start/finish; procurement lead time for X |
| Labour | Per-person time, required skills, ordinary/overtime/agency basis | Quote/rate card and team specification; identify council supervision still needed |
| Equipment | Own/hire status, reservation, operating cost, delivery/operator and maintenance | Included plant versus separately charged items, minimum hire and breakdown cover |
| Materials/disposal | Bill of quantities, usable stock, replacement value, waste treatment and transport | Inclusions, quantities, waste responsibility, variations and disposal charges |
| Additional costs | Permits, traffic management, setup, out-of-hours, access and inspection | Same items plus call-out/minimum order, mobilisation, procurement, assurance and retained council work |
| Quality/lifecycle | Expected service life, repeat work, maintenance and reinstatement | Equivalent performance, guarantee/warranty, defects liability and call-back costs |
| Funding | Eligible pool, incremental spend and opportunity cost tracked separately | Accepted quote basis, VAT, commitments, eligible pool and payment/variation terms |
| Uncertainty | Duration/cost range and evidence of achievable outcome | Quote validity, conditional access, price range, supplier acceptance and contingency |

Do not infer a skilled operative rate from a directorate's salary budget/FTE:
those figures include different grades and functions. Do not assume a contracted
provider is faster, more reliable or cheaper without mode-specific evidence.

### Keep two cost views

**Additional cash required** is what choosing the option changes in the current
cash budget: overtime/agency, hire, fuel/consumables, purchased materials, gate
fees, contractor invoices, incremental supervision and procurement. Ordinary
salary and fixed retainers already committed do not become extra invoices just
because they are allocated to a job. Apply consistent recoverable-VAT treatment.

**Economic/resource cost** also recognises council labour/equipment consumption
and genuine displaced work. Use a clearly chosen resource-value basis. Do not
add full labour cost and a displacement/replacement charge for the same labour
again. A more expensive external option may preserve scarce internal capacity;
the benefit must be identified, not asserted from backlog size alone.

For the same safe, accepted outcome over a common evaluation horizon:

```text
cash_cost(mode) = additional labour + hire/operating cash + materials + disposal
                  + permits/traffic management + mobilisation/procurement
                  + incremental retained council inspection/supervision

economic_cost(mode) = relevant resource/opportunity cost
                      + evidenced repeat-work/lifecycle cost
                      + admissible monetised delay/disruption cost

economic_advantage_of_external = economic_cost(best feasible I/H)
                                 - economic_cost(best feasible T/X)
```

Use expected repeat-work cost only with defensible probabilities and costs;
otherwise show scenarios. Keep non-monetised benefits—safety, accessibility,
resident disruption, equity, carbon and delivery confidence—visible separately.
Do not convert the ordinal severity index into pounds, injury probabilities or
a fabricated penalty per day. Delay cost must not duplicate the value of the
same displaced work, repeat visit or traffic-management charge elsewhere.

### When to recommend external delivery

1. **Resolve responsibility:** an external-owner asset normally follows R, not
   a council-paid outsourcing decision, unless separate authorised council action
   is needed.
2. **Filter for feasibility:** safety/quality, confirmed deadlines, work scope,
   skills, permits, accepted capacity, procurement authority and eligible funds
   are constraints. A cheap infeasible mode is not an alternative.
3. **If I/H is infeasible but T/X is feasible**, recommend that verified external
   option for service-owner approval and record the reason: specialist skill,
   plant, timing or capacity. Missing evidence alone means **seek confirmation**,
   not proof the council cannot do the job.
4. **If both are feasible**, compare additional cash, resource/lifecycle value
   and non-monetised outcomes. A positive economic advantage supports external
   delivery only if its extra cash is affordable and the result is robust to
   plausible estimates. Otherwise show the tradeoff for a human decision.
5. **If neither is feasible**, escalate the capacity/funding/specification gap;
   do not lower severity or silently extend a confirmed deadline.
6. **If ranges overlap materially**, obtain a quote or better work estimate.
   Do not impose an unsupported universal “outsource above £X” threshold.

Emergency protection never waits for a full cost-benefit exercise. Use the duty
service and fastest competent authorised option, retain the decision trail,
and compare subsequent permanent-repair options separately. Hiring equipment or
targeted overflow support may be better than outsourcing an entire case.

### Worked scenario — fictional values, not Hackney rates

Assume a small civil job with an approved equal-quality outcome, all routes and
permissions confirmed **for this fictional scenario**, no deadline conflict and
no existing applicable retainer. The in-house civil team itself is an assumption,
not a claim that Hackney has such a crew. Amounts use one consistent VAT basis.

| Cost / timing | I/H: council staff plus hired plant | T/X: accepted external package |
|---|---:|---:|
| Council operative resource value | 2 people × 4 hours × £35 = £280 | Included in package |
| Job-specific plant hire | £100 | Included in package |
| Materials | £60 | Included in package |
| Other job-specific disposal/site costs | £80 | Included in package except traffic management below |
| External package | — | £350 |
| External traffic management | — | £100 |
| Incremental council oversight (assumed paid overtime) | Included in staff time above | £50 |
| **Additional cash** | **£240**, assuming the £280 ordinary salary is already committed | **£500** |
| **Resource cost before delay** | **£520** | **£500** |
| Completion from decision time | 2 days | 0.5 day |

If a decision-maker uses a **fictional £75/day disruption value solely for
sensitivity**, the totals are £670 versus £537.50: external economic advantage
£132.50, while requiring £260 more additional cash. This does **not** establish
that outsourcing is affordable or preferred under actual council policy.

With zero monetised delay cost, the resource-cost advantage is only £20.
Holding external add-ons at £150, the external package breaks even at £370
without delay valuation, or £482.50 with this fictional delay valuation. A
£250–£550 illustrative package range therefore crosses both thresholds: the
decision needs an actual quote and sensitivity review. No quoted example value
is a benchmark, live rate, approved budget or automatic outsourcing trigger.

For longer-lived interventions, compare lifecycle/repeat visits and residual
condition over the same period. A cheap temporary patch and a durable repair are
different outcomes; represent an immediate make-safe plus later permanent repair
as a combined option rather than declaring the temporary patch better value.

## What the allocator can use now

Use the public register to define **resource types and capability constraints**:
highway inspection, civil repair, surfacing, electrical lighting, gully cleansing,
arboriculture, cleansing/removal, enforcement, structural inspection and housing
trades. Keep action tasks such as inspect, make safe and permanently repair
separate, as in the severity model.

Use published annual figures for context and scenario checks. A real schedule
needs verified capacity over its actual planning window. Do not turn FTE into
available hours by multiplying by an assumed working week. If a resource is
unknown, flag the affected task as capacity-unconfirmed and retain it in the
backlog; do not treat it as safely schedulable or impossible forever.

Council resources, contracted capacity and external handoffs are different:
TfL, Thames Water and other asset owners cannot be scheduled as council crews.
Record the handoff/acceptance and its separate response commitment instead.

These are a proposed data request, not a message sent to the council. Obtain
team-level data first; personal employee details are unnecessary for the demo.

Recommended operational record fields:

```text
resource_id, resource_type, service_owner, provider, capabilities,
quantity, unit, available_from, available_until, already_committed,
emergency_reserve, location_or_depot, eligible_work, funding_pool_id,

```

For time-based resources, compute usable capacity from verified shift time less
bookings, breaks, travel/setup and reserves **in the same units**, without counting
one crew's component staff as additional independent capacity. For budgets,
reconcile actuals and commitments before computing a remaining balance. An
unverified overlapping annual budget is not a substitute for that calculation.

## Research limits

Public evidence has not established current highway crew counts, daily productive
hours, stores inventories, individual vehicle availability, contractor spare slots,
or uncommitted November budget. Staffing is budgeted establishment; announcements
are plans until delivery is verified. No fictional operational quantities have
been filled into the operational resource records. The illustrative work units
and worked costing scenario are explicitly fictional and separate. No allocator,
procurement integration or operational outsourcing decision was implemented.
